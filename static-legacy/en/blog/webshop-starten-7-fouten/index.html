Starting a webshop? 7 mistakes entrepreneurs make (and how to avoid them)

Starting a webshop sounds simple. Choose a platform, upload products, go live. But reality is tougher: research shows that more than 70% of new webshops shut down within two years.

Not because the product is bad. Not because there's no market. But because fundamental mistakes are made during setup that are hard to fix later.

These are the seven mistakes I encounter most often — and how to avoid them.

Mistake 1: Starting without a clear target audience

"Everyone" is not a target audience. Yet I see it regularly: a webshop built for everyone that appeals to no one.

What goes wrong: Product descriptions are generic, categories are too broad, and the shop competes on price with Bol.com and Amazon — a fight you can't win.

How to avoid it: Choose a niche and become the best in it. A timber yard focused on garden wood for homeowners in Drenthe is more findable than a "wood shop" that sells everything to everyone.

Ask yourself these questions:

  • Who is my ideal customer? (Age, region, situation)
  • What problem do I solve for them?
  • Why do they buy from me and not from a marketplace?

Mistake 2: Choosing the wrong platform

The platform choice is one of the most important decisions you make. And it's hard to switch later without rebuilding everything.

The options at a glance:

Platform Suitable for Cost/month Flexibility
Shopify Quick start, little technical knowledge €30 – €300 Limited
WooCommerce (WordPress) More control, existing WP site €10 – €50 (hosting) Good
Custom Specific requirements, unique flows One-time + hosting Unlimited
Lightspeed Dutch market, POS integration €50 – €200 Moderate

Common mistake: Choosing the cheapest platform without thinking about growth. If you have 500 products in a year and need integrations with your accounting software, you'll hit a wall on a platform that wasn't built for that.

How to avoid it: Make a list of what you need now and what you think you'll need in 2 years. Choose the platform that fits both.

Mistake 3: Underestimating product photography

Selling online is about trust. And trust starts with what the customer sees. Blurry photos with a messy background scream "not professional" — regardless of how good your product is.

What you need at minimum:

  • White or neutral background
  • Multiple angles per product
  • Detail photos of material and finish
  • Consistent style throughout the shop

You don't need to hire a photographer. A modern smartphone, a white background, and daylight already deliver good results. But take the time — the difference between "quickly snapped" and "properly photographed" is the difference between selling and not selling.

Mistake 4: Forgetting SEO during the build

Many webshops are built with focus on how they look, not on how they're found. Only after launch does thinking about Google begin — and then it's expensive to fix things.

SEO basics that must be right during the build:

  • URL structure: /category/product-name/ is better than /product?id=12847
  • Page titles and meta descriptions: unique per product, with keywords
  • Product descriptions: original text, not the standard manufacturer description
  • Images: alt text on every photo, optimized file size
  • Speed: under 2 seconds load time
  • Structured data: Product schema with price, availability, and reviews

That last point matters: with the right structured data, search results show your price and rating directly — that dramatically increases your click-through rate.

Mistake 5: Not showing trust signals

An unknown webshop has to work harder to earn trust. Visitors think (consciously or not): "Is this a real store? Will I get my order? Can I return it?"

Trust signals you need:

  • ✅ KvK number (Chamber of Commerce) and VAT number visible
  • ✅ Clear return policy
  • ✅ Familiar payment methods (iDEAL, credit card, pay later)
  • ✅ Customer reviews (Google Reviews, Trustpilot, Kiyoh)
  • ✅ Certification (Thuiswinkel Waarborg, Trusted Shops)
  • ✅ Reachable phone number and address

Every missing trust signal is a reason for a visitor not to order.

Mistake 6: Treating launch as the finish line

The website is live — done! Right? No. Launch is the starting point, not the finish line.

After launch, the real work begins:

  • First 30 days: Monitor everything. Which pages are visited? Where do visitors drop off? Does checkout run smoothly?
  • First 90 days: Optimize based on data. Adjust product descriptions, test different photos, improve the checkout flow.
  • Ongoing: Write blog posts for SEO, maintain social media, test ads, improve customer service.

Budget 15–20 hours per week in the startup phase. Running a webshop isn't a side project.

Mistake 7: Unrealistic expectations

"Once the webshop is live, customers will come on their own." This is the biggest misconception. A new webshop is invisible — to Google, to social media, to everyone.

Realistic expectations:

  • Month 1–3: Hardly any organic traffic. You depend on ads, social media, and your own network.
  • Month 3–6: First organic visitors if your SEO is in order. Slow growth.
  • Month 6–12: Growing traffic, first returning customers, recognizable patterns in data.
  • Year 2+: Stable traffic and revenue if you've worked consistently.

10% monthly growth is realistic and good. Don't expect explosive growth in the first months.

Checklist: are you ready to start?

  • I have defined a clear target audience
  • I have chosen the right platform for my situation
  • I have professional product photos
  • The webshop is technically ready for SEO
  • I show trust signals (KvK, reviews, certification)
  • I have a plan for after launch
  • I have realistic expectations about growth and time investment

Summary

A successful webshop doesn't start with the platform — it starts with preparation. Choose a niche, invest in photos and SEO, show trust, and make a plan for the long term. The webshops that survive aren't always the prettiest or cheapest — they're the best prepared.
