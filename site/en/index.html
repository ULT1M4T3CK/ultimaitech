MKB Digital Flow

Four steps, each with work you can check

Every project runs the same four steps — MKB is the Dutch term for SME. No big bang, and no five-figure quote before anyone knows whether it works.

  1. 1. Intake & stack review

    Processes, people and existing tools mapped. If a smaller fix or standard SaaS is enough, I say so.

    At Sparklezz Beauty: the old site did not put booking in the content. First we look at what is already there, then the calendar goes in the right place.

  2. 2. Quick-win pilot

    The smallest useful step: a booking layer or a website that books. Fixed price, clear scope, real tasks — not demo theatre.

    At Sparklezz Beauty: the online calendar went into the calls to action, not as a loose widget at the bottom.

  3. 3. Evaluation & ROI check

    Does it work in practice? Less manual work, fewer errors, faster follow-up? Only then do we choose the next phase.

    At Nijenhuis Botenverhuur: before — an outdated site with broken links, where bookings partly ran through loose emails and phone calls. After — the information architecture was rebuilt around one conversion: completing a reservation.

  4. 4. Phased rollout

    Extend in modules. Training, documentation and handover belong with every delivery, so you are not locked in.

    At Sparklezz Beauty: before — a standard theme with result photos scattered around and booking not built into the content. After — before-and-after sliders and the online calendar integrated into the calls to action, added module by module.
